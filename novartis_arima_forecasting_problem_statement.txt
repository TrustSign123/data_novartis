NOVARTIS STUDENT LAB
TIME-SERIES FORECASTING WITH ARIMA
==================================

IMPORTANT DATA NOTE
-------------------
This dataset is SYNTHETIC and created for educational/training purposes.
It is not Novartis proprietary data, does not represent actual Novartis
products, patients, sales, markets, or commercial performance, and must not
be interpreted as real pharmaceutical business data.

1. BUSINESS CONTEXT
-------------------
A pharmaceutical commercial analytics team wants to improve its ability to
forecast daily product demand. Accurate demand forecasting can support
inventory planning, supply-chain decisions, commercial planning, and
resource allocation.

You have been provided with 600 days of synthetic pharmaceutical sales data.
Your task is to investigate the historical time series and build an ARIMA-based
forecasting solution for future daily sales.

The key business question is:

    "Based on historical daily sales behavior, what sales volume should the
     business expect over the next forecasting period?"

2. DATASET
----------
File:
    novartis_time_series_pharma_sales_600_rows.csv

Number of observations:
    600 daily observations

Date range:
    2019-01-01 through 2020-08-22

Columns:
    Date
        Observation date.

    Product
        Synthetic product identifier. Included to resemble a commercial
        product dataset.

    Region
        Synthetic business region: North, South, East, or West.

    Sales_Units
        Daily number of product units sold. THIS IS THE PRIMARY TARGET
        VARIABLE FOR FORECASTING.

    Price_USD
        Synthetic average selling price per unit in USD.

    Marketing_Spend_USD
        Synthetic daily marketing spend in USD.

    Promotion
        Binary indicator:
            0 = no promotion
            1 = promotion active

    Inventory_Units
        Synthetic available inventory.

    Day_of_Week
        Name of the day corresponding to Date.

3. YOUR OBJECTIVE
-----------------
Build a time-series forecasting model that predicts future Sales_Units.

You should use ARIMA as the main forecasting method.

Do NOT randomly shuffle the time-series observations.

Your solution should demonstrate that you understand:

    - time-series structure
    - trend
    - seasonality
    - stationarity
    - differencing
    - ARIMA(p,d,q)
    - temporal train/test splitting
    - forecasting
    - forecast evaluation
    - residual analysis

4. EXPECTED ANALYSIS
--------------------

STEP 1 — Load the data
    - Import pandas.
    - Read the CSV.
    - Inspect shape, columns, data types, and missing values.
    - Convert Date to datetime.
    - Sort the data by Date.
    - Set Date as the time-series index.

STEP 2 — Exploratory analysis
    - Plot Sales_Units against Date.
    - Describe the overall trend.
    - Investigate whether sales show weekly patterns.
    - Check whether promotions appear associated with higher sales.
    - Inspect the distribution of Sales_Units.
    - Calculate basic descriptive statistics.

STEP 3 — Understand time-series components
    Discuss whether the series appears to contain:
        - level
        - trend
        - seasonality
        - noise

    Explain your observations from the plot rather than simply stating
    definitions.

STEP 4 — Check stationarity
    Use an appropriate statistical test such as the Augmented Dickey-Fuller
    (ADF) test.

    Answer:
        - Is the original Sales_Units series stationary?
        - What does the test statistic mean?
        - What does the p-value indicate?
        - Is differencing required?

STEP 5 — Differencing
    If appropriate, apply first-order differencing.

    Compare:
        Original series
        vs.
        Differenced series

    Explain why differencing can help ARIMA model a non-stationary series.

STEP 6 — Understand ACF and PACF
    Generate ACF and PACF plots.

    Use them as diagnostic tools to develop candidate values for:
        p
        d
        q

    Do not blindly select parameters only from the plots. Explain that
    model selection should also consider validation performance and
    diagnostics.

STEP 7 — Build ARIMA models
    Build several candidate ARIMA models, for example:

        ARIMA(1,1,0)
        ARIMA(1,1,1)
        ARIMA(2,1,0)
        ARIMA(2,1,1)
        ARIMA(3,1,1)

    You may test additional reasonable combinations.

STEP 8 — Temporal train/test split
    Use an order-preserving split.

    Suggested approach:
        Training set = first 80% of observations
        Test set     = final 20% of observations

    IMPORTANT:
        Never use random train_test_split(shuffle=True) for this task.

STEP 9 — Forecast the test period
    Fit the ARIMA model using the training data only.

    Forecast exactly the length of the test set.

    Compare:
        Actual Sales_Units
        vs.
        Forecast Sales_Units

STEP 10 — Evaluate performance
    Calculate at least:

        MAE
        RMSE

    Optionally calculate:
        MAPE

    Explain what each metric means in business terms.

    Example interpretation:
        "The model's MAE of X means that the forecast was off by
         approximately X units per day on average."

STEP 11 — Compare models
    Create a comparison table containing:

        Model
        MAE
        RMSE
        Optional MAPE

    Select the best model based on validation performance, while also
    considering model simplicity and diagnostic quality.

STEP 12 — Residual diagnostics
    Analyze the residuals:

        Residual = Actual - Forecast

    Check whether residuals:
        - have a mean close to zero
        - show remaining autocorrelation
        - show obvious patterns
        - contain unusually large errors

    Explain why a good forecasting model should leave mostly unpredictable
    residuals.

STEP 13 — Final forecasting model
    After selecting a suitable ARIMA specification, refit the chosen model
    using all available historical observations.

    Forecast the next 30 days.

    Produce:
        Date
        Forecast_Sales_Units

STEP 14 — Visualization
    Create a final chart showing:

        Historical Sales
        Test-period Forecast
        Future 30-day Forecast

    Clearly label the actual and forecast portions.

5. BUSINESS INTERPRETATION
--------------------------
Answer the following questions in your final notebook:

    1. What is the overall sales trend?

    2. Is there evidence of recurring seasonality?

    3. Was differencing necessary? Why?

    4. Which ARIMA(p,d,q) model performed best?

    5. What were the MAE and RMSE?

    6. How would you explain the model's accuracy to a non-technical
       commercial or supply-chain stakeholder?

    7. What are the limitations of forecasting daily pharmaceutical sales
       using only historical sales?

    8. Which external variables in the dataset could potentially explain
       sales changes that pure ARIMA cannot directly capture?

6. IMPORTANT CONCEPT — ARIMA LIMITATION
---------------------------------------
Standard ARIMA primarily models the target series' own temporal behavior.

The dataset contains additional variables such as:
    - Price_USD
    - Marketing_Spend_USD
    - Promotion
    - Inventory_Units
    - Region

Do not automatically put all of these variables into standard ARIMA.

Discuss why a model such as ARIMAX/SARIMAX or a feature-based machine-learning
model could be considered when external predictors are important.
